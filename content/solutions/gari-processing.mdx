## The problem

Grating by hand is the slowest and hardest step in making gari, and it limits how much cassava a group can process before the roots spoil. A motorised grater turns peeled cassava into fine wet mash far faster.

## What you need

The cassava grater is the machine we list for this work. It also suits agbelima, kokonte and cassava starch. The other steps, pressing, sieving and roasting, are often done with simple local equipment. If you would like a machine for one of those steps, ask our sales team and they will tell you what is possible.

## Power options

- **Electric motor:** a good choice at a fixed site with a reliable supply.
- **Petrol engine:** lets you work where there is no mains power, or move between sites.

## Space and power considerations

Gari processing is wet work, so plan for drainage and clean water for washing. Keep the roasting area separate from the grater and well ventilated. Peel and grate cassava soon after harvest, because the roots spoil quickly. Keep hands clear of the grating drum while it runs, and wash the machine after every use.
